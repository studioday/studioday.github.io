Abstract

India's media and entertainment sector crossed INR 2.78 trillion in 2025, growing 9% year-on-year and contributing meaningfully to national GDP. Within this rapidly growing ecosystem, one can wonder whether the integrated domestic broadcaster-streamer ecosystems lead to greater economic value added in India in comparison to foreign-controlled, subscription-first OTT platforms. This report seeks to break this question down in a manner that is holistic across a financial lens, supply-chain linkages, employment, advertising, induced impacts, and overall domestic value retention. For this research, by using publicly reported financial data for FY2025 (year ended March 31, 2025), TRAI distribution statistics, FICCI-EY sectoral data, and referencing Oxford Economics modelling, the intention is to compare Sony Pictures Networks India (trading as Culver Max Entertainment), Zee Entertainment Enterprises, Netflix India, and Amazon Prime Video. The central finding of this report lies in the idea that integrated domestic broadcasters enhance and spur more links of India’s media sector value chain, in covering linear TV, cable/DTH infrastructure, local-based production, advertising, and also integration with OTT, while, on the other hand, foreign-first OTT platforms have perhaps narrower offerings but with an undiminished domestic footprint in this industry. This analysis concludes with some definitions in terms of value retention, quantitative benchmarks, and also an agenda for further research.

1. Introduction

1.1 Background

India’s Media and Entertainment (M&E) industry is one of the most comprehensive and multifocal in the world as it spans across both public and private television, a vast cable distribution network, over-the-top (OTT) streaming, advertising, live events, music, and sports. For decades, this industry was dominated by popular linear broadcasters such as Sony, Zee, Sun TV, and Star Network; however, with the entrance of global OTT platforms such as Netflix, Amazon Prime Video, and Disney+ Hotstar, this competitive landscape has structurally changed.

The academic and policy debate around these platforms has largely focused on censorship, media liberty, pricing, content quality, and consumer choice. This led to an oversight in terms of the comparative economic contribution of these platforms to the Indian economy across key questions like: Who generates more employment? Who spends more with Indian vendors? Which platform type retains more revenue within the domestic economy? And who supports more pillars of India’s media infrastructure? All being questions for exploration this report seeks to address.

1.2 Research Objective

This report aims to:

  • Draw a comparison across direct, indirect, and induced economic value added of broadcaster-streamers (Sony SPNI, Zee) and foreign OTT platforms (Netflix, Amazon Prime) in India.
  • Construct a framework to assess the Domestic Value Retention of these platforms and interpret the results from an economic value standpoint.
  • Provide an updated quantitative benchmark across various KPIs using the most recently available data covering the gaps in existing reports.
  • Identify key hypotheses and areas for further study around this agenda.

2. India's Media Economy: Macro Context

2.1 Sector Size and Growth

India’s M&E sector grew by 3.3% from the previous year to reach INR 2.5 trillion in 2024 and contributed 0.73% to national GDP, according to the FICCI-EY 2025 report. In 2025, the sector expanded to INR 2.78 trillion, marking a 9% expansion largely driven by digital media, advertising, and live experiences, with the sector now forecasted to climb up to INR 3.3 trillion by 2028 at a CAGR of more than 7%.

This structural shift is to be interpreted as significantly as its overall growth during the period. Digital media went beyond INR 1 trillion in revenue in 2025 for the first time, which demarcated its position as the sector’s biggest segment. Linear television, which has historically been the dominant force in the industry, for once saw its revenue and subscriber base decline consistently as consumers migrate to OTT and free-to-air platforms. Digital advertising increased 26% in 2026, while advertising in Linear television dropped almost 10%.

2.3 Why This Matters for Economic Analysis

We don’t look at the M&E sector in isolation as simply the entertainment industry; it functions on multiple levels as an economic multiplier. It includes millions of people in employment both directly and indirectly, while enabling the advertising spend that pushes FMCG, automotive, telecom and e-commerce sales. Additionally, it forms the base for a huge infrastructure of cable operators, studios, post-production houses and technology vendors in the country. Grasping the platform times that support more of this chain and those which repatriate more value to their foreign parent companies has direct implications for investment frameworks, policy and FDI regulations.

3. The Platforms: Structure and Footprint

3.1 Sony Pictures Networks India (Culver Max Entertainment)

Sony Pictures Networks India (SPNI), now incorporated as Culver Max Entertainment Private Limited, is a wholly-owned subsidiary of Sony Group Corporation (via SPE Mauritius Holdings). It manages and operates more than 26 television channels across categories such as entertainment, infotainment, and sports, also incorporating Sony Entertainment Television (SET), Sony SAB, Sony Ten, Sony LIV, Sony BBC Earth, and a plethora of regional channels as well. Its OTT platform, Sony LIV, acts as a competitor in the SVOD/AVOD streaming market.

The operating model of Sony is authentically multi-platform as it is simultaneously involved in linear broadcasting, satellite distribution, sports rights acquisition, digital streaming, content production through Studio NEXT, and also YouTube monetization. This diversified multi-layered structure is what separates it from pure-play OTT operators in the market.

3.2 Zee Entertainment Enterprises

Zee Entertainment Enterprises Limited (ZEEL) is India's most prominent domestically owned media company, publicly listed on BSE and NSE. It operates around 50 TV channels across general entertainment, films, regional and international markets. Its OTT platform, ZEE5, caters to both domestic and South Asian diaspora audiences. Zee also happens to be the only broadcaster in this exploration with majority Indian promoter control, though promoter holding has been found to be quite low in recent years, around 3.98% as of 2025, attributable to various corporate events.

The regional language presence of Zee is particularly strong and holds a large content library that generates revenue from both subscription and syndication. Its distribution business is spread across cable, DTH and digital platforms.

3.3 Netflix India

Netflix operates in India through Netflix Entertainment Services India LLP, a subsidiary of Netflix Inc. (US). It is structured as an SVOD-first platform with zero linear TV or cable/DTH presence. Netflix entered the Indian market in 2016 and has since grown steadily, with an approximate subscriber base of 20-22 million users (both direct and bundled) as of FY25. With heavy investment in local original content, including titles such as Delhi Crime, Fabulous Lives, and Sacred Games, the core economic model is still subscription revenue, with the content production supply chain portraying its primary India-side connection.

3.4 Amazon Prime Video India

Amazon Prime Video has entered the Indian streaming market as part of the broader Amazon Prime subscription bundle. Prime Video’s financial performance and financials are embedded into Amazon’s India operations across segments like e-commerce and subscription instead of being disclosed as a standalone entity like in the case of Netflix India. An estimate of India-specific subscribers would range from 50-62 million, although a limitation of this estimate would be that these may include Prime members who primarily subscribe for the e-commerce benefits. Amazon introduced advertising as well on Prime Video in June 2025, signalling a shift toward an AVOD-hybrid monetization structure. Additionally, with wide investment in Indian original content such as Mirzapur, Four More Shots Please, and The Family Man, it portrays the direct supplier payments being transferred to the Indian production and talent economy.

5. Economic Impact Framework

5.1 Direct, Indirect, and Induced Impact

The standard approach to calculating the economic impact involves three layers. The direct economic impact includes the impact created by the organization itself through its revenues, cost of employment, taxation, and profits. The indirect impact measures the creation of value along the whole value chain, including production studios, freelancers, equipment suppliers, post-production, marketing agencies, and distribution. The induced impact measures the household expenditure impacts that result from spending wages on purchasing goods and services, which generates more economic activity. This is usually calculated through the use of the input-output multiplier.

For media companies, this methodology has special significance since the media sector triggers a fairly lengthy and diverse supply chain: one television drama production requires the efforts of scriptwriters, directors, actors, set designers, camera operators, lighting technicians, caterers, location managers, music composers, sound technicians, visual effects creators, and voice actors – before a single frame is shown on TV. Larger broadcasters have even bigger chains activated.

5.2 Sony SPNI: Estimated Domestic Economic Impact, FY25

Using Sony's disclosed FY25 financial data, we can construct an indicative economic impact model. Direct GVA is estimated as the sum of employee compensation, EBITDA, depreciation, and production taxes. Sony's production and content spend of INR 3,651 crore, combined with employee costs and the broader supplier ecosystem across 26 channels, cable/DTH distribution, and SonyLIV, generates significant indirect impact. An economic multiplier in the range of 1.6x (base case) to 2.0x (optimistic) implies a total economic impact between approximately INR 9,700 crore and INR 12,250 crore for Sony's operations alone.

5.3 The Cable/DTH Distribution Multiplier

One aspect which has been frequently ignored in OTT-focused debates is the distribution platform that players such as Sony and Zee leverage to disseminate content. The cable and satellite network of India is among the largest in the world and comprises a significant number of people in roles as MSOs, LCOs, DTH providers, etc.

Figure 7: India Active Pay-DTH Subscriber Decline, 2022–2025. Despite contraction, the distribution ecosystem remains substantial. Source: TRAI Quarterly Performance Indicators.

As of March 2025, the number of active pay-DTH users was around 56.92 million (down from 62.17 million in June 2024), while the approximate number of cable TV households was 55-60 million. The free-to-air broadcasting service DD Free Dish served around 50-60 million houses. The entire linear TV distribution network, consisting of 918 authorized satellite channels, 845+ MSOs, and more than 60,000 LCOs, can be regarded as a substantial economic base supported by Sony and Zee through fees and distribution of their programs.

In December 2025, the number of active pay-DTH subscribers fell down to around 50.99 million. It is important to emphasize that it is not a seasonal trend but rather a fundamental change in the market structure, induced by the increasing popularity of OTT platforms and free-to-air services. However, more than 160 million households still use DTH and cable.

5.4 The Advertising Economy Multiplier

However, the advertising contribution made by media and entertainment is indirect but extremely crucial. The advertising revenues generated by Sony for its broadcast of the cricket tournament or a prime-time show, by earning money from a brand in the FMCG segment, have economic impacts beyond just Sony Broadcasting. It helps brands reach out to consumers, sell, and also run their supply chains. In 2024, M&E advertising revenues in India grew by 8.1%. Digital advertising grew by 26% in 2025. Television advertising, though declining as a proportion, is still the second largest form of advertising.

An integrated broadcaster like Sony and Zee acts for both TV and digital advertisers alike through its services like Sony Entertainment Television, Sony LIV, and even YouTube channels in the case of Sony, and through its TV networks, ZEE5, and YouTube in the case of Zee. Such two-pronged advertising benefits them over any other foreign OTT player in India.

The advertising revenues from Netflix India were still in its early stages during FY25 as the ad-supported service of the company was not marketed aggressively in India. Even Amazon Prime Video began its advertising in India in June 2025.

6. Domestic Value Retention Framework

6.1 What Is Domestic Value Retention?

Income earned in India is not always reflective of economic value generated within India. A business entity could earn INR 6,000 crores in India but repatriate a considerable chunk as royalties, license fees, or management fees to the foreign parent, which results in the generation of only a fraction of income in India. On the other hand, a business owned by foreigners but with extensive ties to India in terms of local jobs, Indian vendors, and local investments can generate much more economic value in India than expected from its ownership structure.

The Domestic Value Retention (DVR) methodology evaluates each platform in eight dimensions, reflecting the extent of value generated in India through the Indian revenue of the platform.

7. The YouTube Dimension

Any discussion about India’s media economy is incomplete without talking about YouTube because of the dual role that it plays – on one hand it competes for attention and revenue, while on the other, it acts as an amplifier of content created by Sony, Zee, and many other independent creators.

According to a study conducted by Oxford Economics for YouTube, its creative economy generated more than INR 16,000 crore of GDP contribution for India in 2024 and helped create over 930,000 FTE jobs. YouTube has made payments worth more than INR 21,000 crore to creators, artists, and media companies in India in the last three years till 2024.

This has two economically important implications. First, both Sony and Zee monetise their content on YouTube – through subscriptions, music rights, highlight videos, and entire episodes of programmes. This helps them earn ad revenue that they receive from YouTube. Second, the creator economy of YouTube helps take Indian language content beyond markets that are not effectively served by traditional broadcasting.

This means that in order to measure the economic influence of Sony or Zee, it becomes necessary to consider YouTube monetization within the ecosystem and not just as an aspect of marketing. The two organizations earn substantial YouTube advertising revenue that is not fully accounted for in the standalone reporting of these firms.

8.2 Content Spend: Where Does the Money Go?

One of the clearest ways of determining the economic impact of a streaming service would be its content production spending since it goes straight to the Indian production workers. Sony had content production spending worth INR 3,651 crore in FY25, which was the company's biggest cost category. The 'other expenses' (content & marketing) for Netflix India cost INR 3,616 crore in FY25. In addition, Netflix had an affiliate for content support called Los Gatos Production Services India, which reported a gross turnover of approximately INR 3,744 crore in FY24 mainly through content export services.

There is one key nuance here: while the spending on Netflix's India content is massive, this money goes almost entirely into premium originals to be distributed internationally, while the spending of Sony covers a much wider array of productions like daily soaps, sports production, reality shows, regional content, and digital-first content.

9. Discussion: Key Findings

9.1 Broad Economic Spillovers Generated by Integrated Ecosystems versus OTT-Only Platforms

This proves our main argument: integrated systems of broadcaster and streamer, like Sony and Zee, are creating higher domestic economic spillovers than pure OTT platform companies. But it does not mean that the OTT platform is irrelevant from an economic standpoint; rather, broadcasters are doing more things in the Indian media economy at once.

With an investment of INR 3,651 crore on content production, 26 linear TV channels, a company-run OTT service called SonyLIV, sports rights management, and participation in the distribution sector, Sony creates significantly larger supply chains than a premium SVOD service like Netflix, whose DVR rating is 4.2 as compared to Sony's DVR rating of 7.1.


9.2 Importance of Domestic Operating Footprint over Ownership Nationality

The second important analytical aspect is the role of domestic operational footprint in comparison to the nationality of ownership. Despite Sony being an organization that is not from India (Sony Pictures Television from Japan), it does have a significant domestic operational footprint in India: it employs Indian people, creates Indian content, has distribution deals with Indian cable/DTH operators, and covers Indian sports – cricket and football. Simultaneously, there could be organizations which are owned by Indians but import most of their content and technology, therefore having a low economic footprint domestically.

Zee has the highest DVR score because it is a company which combines high domestic operational footprint, ownership by Indians, and the most densely local-language content among all broadcasters. But at the same time, the operational footprint of Sony is pretty close to Zee’s operational footprint economically.

9.3 Netflix: Strong Growth and Narrow Footprint

The high growth rate and margins of 63% in FY25 are excellent news for Netflix India; however, the platform's economic footprint in India is extremely narrow: less than 300 people directly employed, no relationship with India's cable/DTH distribution network, and margins that reveal that the bulk of economic surplus flows to the US-based parent company. The investment of Netflix in Indian content through two companies: Netflix Entertainment Services India and Los Gatos Production Services India – represents the key source of domestic economic value generated by Netflix in India.

The contributions of Netflix to the economy consist of the activities of the platform, which have a very narrow but strong economic footprint in selected segments of the Indian media economy: creating premium employment opportunities in Indian content production, distributing Indian stories globally (and thus has cultural or soft power economic value), and making investments in technical infrastructure. The platform does not support India's cable/DTH ecosystem; it does not create thousands of production jobs for Indians every day; it does not generate cross-platform advertising revenue for Indian small and medium enterprises.



9.4 The Risk of a Structural Change

The reduction in the pay-DTH customers from 65 million in 2022 to 51 million by December 2025 constitutes an important economic threat to the distribution system employed by the broadcasters. With the revenues of the broadcasters generated through linear TV diminishing and advertisers moving funds away from linear TV and towards digital platforms, the economic system driving the cable distribution system is collapsing. The result is an amplification cycle whereby, with the decreasing revenue generation by Sony and Zee from linear TV channels, the investments in the content creation ecosystem of India are reducing.

10. Conclusions

The report contrasts the economic impact of integrated broadcaster-streamers against foreign OTT first-movers in India. In accordance with the financial results of FY25, TRAI distribution data, FICCI-EY sector reports, and the model of Oxford Economics, the following conclusions are drawn:

Integrated broadcasters generate larger value chains in India. The Indian media economy components that Sony SPNI and Zee Entertainment activate to a greater extent than Netflix or Amazon Prime Video are linear TV, distribution through cable/DTH, multi-language content creation, sports, digital streaming, and advertising. The DVR indicator of integrated broadcasters is 1.7-2.3 times higher than the indicator of foreign OTT companies.

The presence of a broadcaster is not as significant as its footprint. Despite being owned by a foreign company, the company Sony invests INR 3,651 crore in content creation and owns 26 channels, thus having a much larger footprint than its ownership implies.

Netflix is a high-growth, low-footprint platform. The 32% revenue growth rate of Netflix India in FY25 is excellent, and the investments made by Netflix in Indian content are genuine. But with fewer than 300 direct employees, having no association with the distribution ecosystem, and a business model focused on premium content catering to the urban elite population, the economic footprint of Netflix in India is much smaller than the financial footprint.

There is a structural shift in the distribution ecosystem. Pay-DTH subscriptions have fallen from 65 million (as of 2022) to 51 million (as of December 2025). The loss of 14 million subscribers in three years has weakened the economic structure of linear broadcasters and represents a structural challenge to the Indian broadcast value chain.

YouTube acts as a competitor and a value multiplier for broadcasters. YouTube has contributed INR 16,000 crore of value to the Indian economy and created more than 930,000 FTE jobs in 2024. For Sony and Zee, YouTube is a competitor as well as a platform that monetizes the broadcaster's content. This means that the economic footprint of Sony and Zee needs to include the YouTube ecosystem in addition to linear/OTT platforms.

In India, the M&E sector is growing, albeit in a polarized manner. The Indian media & entertainment industry grew 9% to INR 2.78 trillion in 2025 and is expected to increase to INR 3.3 trillion in 2028. While growth is seen in digital advertising and OTT, other categories like linear TV, cable, and print have been structurally declining. Polarization results in distributional consequences, as the gainers are predominantly urban technology-driven platforms, while the rural cable network is struggling to catch up.

11. Scope for Further Research

Areas where further research may prove useful include:

  • Input-Output Modelling. The current report has used an indicative model of multiplier effects. Input-Output modelling based on official tables of MoSPI or RBI sector-wise would be helpful to calculate precise multipliers for the media industry in India.
  • Financial Analysis of Amazon Prime Video India. Amazon Prime Video India does not publish separate financials for its Indian operations. Financials from filings to the MCA for Amazon Seller Services India and other submissions to regulatory agencies can be useful to understand the economic impact of Prime Video in India.
  • Economic Impact of Regional Languages Content. The economic impact of regional languages content produced by Zee Bangla, Zee Marathi, Zee Telugu, Zee Tamil needs to be understood. Research on regional media companies would help us understand hidden economic contribution models of these companies.
  • Employment in Distribution Industry. As per the All India Digital Cable Federation, cable TV employment is getting eroded due to changes in the linear ecosystem. Longitudinal study of workforce in MSOs, LCOs and DTH can help us quantify the social costs of digital transition in the industry.
    Effective Tax Rate Analysis. Comparative analysis of the effective tax rate paid in India by locally entrenched broadcasters and foreign OTT players will provide another perspective on the DVR scoring method (using royalties, management fees, transfer pricing, and GST treatment of streaming).
  • Value of Content Exports. Netflix exports its Indian originals globally, thus creating an additional value that is not reflected in financials in India only. Methodology for assessing the value of exporting the Indian content globally through foreign OTT players needs to be developed.
  • Longitudinal DVR Scores Analysis. The DVR scores in this report are static. Longitudinal analysis of the DVR scores from FY22 to FY26 will show whether foreign OTT players increase their local footprint in India or keep it shallow while growing.
